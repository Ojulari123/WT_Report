# Work Term Report Websites — Research Brief

## Who actually requires a *website* (not a PDF)

**University of Guelph, School of Computer Science** is the clearest precedent: the co-op
work report is explicitly submitted "in the form of a website or blog that serves as a
portfolio of student capabilities and experiences." A URL is submitted via CourseLink.
Source: uoguelph.ca/computing — co-op work term reports.

Everyone else (Waterloo, UNB, Regina) still expects a document. So the *website* genre is
small, and the existing examples are weak — which is the opportunity here.

## The canonical section list (Guelph website format)

1. **Abstract / Introduction** — tell the reader what you hope they take away
2. **Employer Information** — company description, its relevance to the computing field,
   interesting facts
3. **Goals** — learning outcomes achieved *and unmet*, skills developed, technology explored
4. **Job Description** — project overview, what was unique about it, skills required
5. **Conclusions** — key takeaways for the audience
6. **Acknowledgments** — supervisors, coordinators, mentors

Constraint: must work across multiple browsers and operating systems.

## Rubric (Guelph, 5 levels)

| Level | What separates it |
|---|---|
| Outstanding | Excellent writing + genuine reflection + **creative imagery** |
| Very Good | Well-written, images present, strong reflection |
| Good | Adequate coverage of work, **minimal reflection** |
| Satisfactory | Brief, limited reflection, basic goal/employer coverage |
| Unsatisfactory | May trigger removal from program |

**The design-relevant finding:** the only things that move a report from "Good" to
"Outstanding" are depth of reflection and *imagery*. A text-only page caps out at "Good."
Visual design is not decoration here — it is a scored criterion.

## Document-format specs, for comparison

- **UNB CS** — structure varies by work term number. Terms 1–2 reflective (Introduction,
  Summary, Professional/Personal Development, + monthly log appendix). Term 3 analytical
  (letter of transmittal, analytical body, references, appendices). Term 4 self-evaluation.
  9–15 pages single-spaced, 4,500–7,500 words. Pass/fail.
  Explicitly prohibited: reference manuals, user guides, unanalyzed technical facts.
  Required: **unity** — mention all work in the intro, then go deep on *one* topic.
- **Waterloo** — Major Reflective Reports (MRR) via PD courses for Arts/Env/Health/Math/Sci;
  program-administered work term reports for some Engineering programs. Structure is
  program-specific (title page, ToC, intro/body/conclusion).
- **Waterloo Economics** — 1,250–1,500 words, due within 10 lecture days of the next term.
- **Northeastern** — no public work-term-report-as-website spec found. Reflection happens via
  written assignments, online reflections, and coordinator check-ins; format is set per
  college/coordinator. Needs confirming with the coordinator directly.

## What existing student sites look like (the bar to beat)

**kboparai.github.io** (Guelph CS) — single long page. Anchor-link table of contents at top,
name/student ID/email in the header, then one block per work term (employer logo, background
facts, job duties as bullets, three numbered goals, conclusion, acknowledgments). Sections
divided by asterisk rules. Assessment: text-heavy, document-on-a-webpage, not visually
considered.

**artsydev.com/f24-work-term-report.html** (Guelph) — stronger. Sits inside a real personal
portfolio (Home / About Me / Projects / Contact / Work Term Reports), so the report benefits
from an existing identity. Single-column, generous whitespace, image dividers, skill
development broken into three named subsections, live links to the four sites she shipped,
footer bio. Substance over decoration.

## Design implications

1. **Structure is fixed, not creative** — the six Guelph sections map to six page sections.
   An advisor grades against that list, so the section names should be legible and findable.
   Anchor navigation is the right pattern.
2. **Reflection needs room to breathe** — "goals achieved and unmet" is where the grade is
   won. The unmet-goals part is what most students bury. Give it real space.
3. **Imagery is scored** — company logos, architecture diagrams, screenshots of shipped work,
   a term timeline. Not stock photography.
4. **It doubles as a portfolio** — the report is read by faculty, future co-op students, and
   potential employers. It should survive being sent to a recruiter.
5. **Confidentiality** — nothing in the Guelph spec, but employer NDAs still apply.
   Describe systems and decisions, not proprietary internals. Screenshots may need redaction.
6. **Multi-term reports need a switcher** — the existing examples stack terms vertically,
   which makes the page enormous. Tabs or separate routes scale better.
